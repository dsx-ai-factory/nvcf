# Restoring a Dynamo worker into a shared namespace as its own identity

Status: built for etcd discovery, behind `agent.identityRestore.enabled`
(default off); not yet run on hardware with this code. Kubernetes discovery is
not built. Scope: `internal/agent/identity_etcd.go`,
`internal/agent/identity_etcd_client.go`, `internal/agent/identity_peer_reset.go`,
and their call sites in `criu_group.go` (`captureGroup`, `checkpointInstance`,
`restoreGroup`, `lookupCRIUGroup`), `criu_auto_restore.go` (`restore`),
`grove_replicas.go` (`sharedCompanionsReason`) and `checkpoint_v2.go` (`dumpV2`).

## 1. Problem

A Dynamo worker computes its identity once at start-up: with etcd discovery,
its instance id is the ID of the etcd lease it registers under, and every key
it writes (endpoint registrations under `v1/instances/`, its model card under
`v1/mdc/`, its event channels under `v1/event_channels/`) is attached to that
lease. A checkpoint copies that identity. When many workers share one etcd,
NATS and frontend, nvsnap cannot capture the registry with one worker, so it
refuses such a worker (`grove_replicas.go` `otherDynamoWorker`, and the
`criu_group.go` refusal of a Dynamo rank with no etcd or NATS in its
namespace): restored, the copy's registration would be either a duplicate (its
source alive) or gone (its lease expired). Every such worker starts cold.

## 2. Idea

Do not change a restored worker's identity: make its own captured identity
valid again before it runs, and never have two live holders of one identity.
etcd lets a client choose the ID of a lease it grants, and refuses the grant
of an ID that is live, so the agent can re-establish the captured lease from
outside the process, and etcd itself rejects a second holder.

## 3. Capture

With the flag on, a candidate (`identityRestoreCandidate`) is a Dynamo
component on the pod network that names a plain-HTTP etcd literally in
`ETCD_ENDPOINTS` and does not use another discovery backend. When the group
capture would refuse it for the companions its namespace shares, or because
no etcd or NATS of its namespace is connected to it, it is captured alone:
companion discovery is skipped, so the shared frontend, etcd and NATS are never
stopped or captured with it.

Before the freeze the leader reads, from the worker's etcd, through the
worker's own network namespace (`podNetnsDialer`):

- every key under `v1/`; the worker's lease is the one lease that holds an
  instance registration named by its own ID in hex and a value naming the
  worker's pod address as a URL host. None, or more than one, refuses the
  capture.
- the lease's granted TTL and every key attached to it, with their values.
  A key whose name, or whose value outside a URL host, names a rank's address
  refuses the capture: a restore could not rewrite it.

The identity (lease ID, TTL, pod address, the slot that holds the lease, the
etcd endpoints and the keys) is written into the group record with the
capture's outcome (`identity.*` keys). None of it is logged.

## 4. Resetting the source's outside connections

CRIU dumps established TCP connections in repair mode and closes them silently
when it kills the dumped tree, and it leaves its network lock (an nftables
table that drops the dumped connections' packets) in the pod's network
namespace. A peer outside the captured group never learns the source is gone:
a shared NATS kept a dead source's subscription for about six minutes in the
spike and handed it part of the restored worker's requests.

After a dump that killed its source (`endPeerReset`), the member's agent:

1. runs `criu net-unlock -D <images>` in the source pod's network namespace,
   which removes the dump's lock (the table is named by the dump's run id in
   the images);
2. for each connection the container had to a peer that is neither loopback
   nor a member of the group (listed before the dump from the container's
   sockets), binds a socket to the connection's own address and port and
   connects to the peer. The peer, still holding the connection, answers the
   SYN with an ACK of its own sequence (RFC 5961); the probe answers that
   with a reset whose sequence is the ACK's, which the peer accepts
   (RFC 793), and drops the connection. The probe is closed.

This needs no sequence numbers, no raw sockets and no change to the dump. A
failure fails the capture (the source is replaced cold), so no identity is
recorded whose source may still hold sessions. The connections listed are the
container's own, both directions: a frontend's connection into the worker on a
TCP request plane is reset too, which both discovery backends need.

## 5. Restore

A placeholder of a record with an identity is restored by the lone path
(`criu_auto_restore.go` `restore`) or the group path (`criu_group.go`
`restoreGroup`); both call `identityBeforeRestore` before anything is
restored, after the checkpoint is local:

1. claim the identity in the record (section 6);
2. wait for the captured lease to expire: at most the recorded TTL and a few
   seconds, since the source is dead. A lease whose TTL is renewed meanwhile,
   or that outlives the wait, is held by a live process: refused;
3. grant the lease with the same ID and TTL (etcd refuses a live ID: refused),
   then write every saved key with the captured pod address replaced by the
   new pod's. A failed write revokes the grant;
4. keep the lease alive from the agent through the restore, renewing it when
   a quarter of its TTL is left. Once the process resumed, the agent watches
   for a renewal it did not make, which is the process's own keep-alive, and
   stops. A process whose keep-alive is not seen within three TTLs (at least a
   minute) has its lease revoked, so the frontend stops routing to a worker
   that does not keep its registration. A restore that fails revokes it.

A refused lone restore starts the pod's workload cold in place (or, when it
cannot, deletes the pod for its controller to replace); nothing is counted
against the capture and no checkpoint is blocked. A refused group restore ends
as not run.

## 6. One live restore per identity

Two guards:

- the record names the restore that holds the identity
  (`identity.holder`, `namespace/pod/uid`), written with the record's
  version, so of two agents claiming at once one wins. A holder whose pod is
  gone or replaced is taken over; one whose pod lives refuses the claim; an
  unreadable holder refuses it (fail closed). A refused or failed restore
  releases its claim;
- etcd refuses a grant of a live lease ID, whatever the record says.

## 7. Flag off, and rollback

With the flag off nothing of this runs: capture and restore behave as before,
and the chart renders as before. A record with an identity, written while the
flag was on, is invisible to admission with the flag off
(`identityRecordVisible`), so its configuration starts cold rather than restore
without its lease. Turning the flag off is the rollback.

## 8. Observability

`nvsnap_identity_restore_decisions_total{decision}` counts each decision:
`captured`, `capture_refused`, `peers_reset`, `peers_reset_failed`,
`refused_live_lease`, `refused_held`, `refused_error`, `refused_disabled`,
`regranted`, `keepalive_handoff`, `keepalive_handoff_timeout`,
`keepalive_lease_lost`, `restore_aborted`, `keeper_agent_stopped`. Every
value exists at zero from the agent's start. Each decision also logs one line
with the group key and reason, never the lease ID, keys or addresses.

## 9. Invariants

Against the invariants in `AGENTS.md`:

1. The source is stopped by its own dump only; the shared companions keep
   running throughout.
2. One restore per pod, and one live restore per identity (section 6).
3. An identity record holds one worker and no companions; admission still
   matches size and process configuration.
5. Every unreadable state refuses: registry, record, holder, lease.
7. The claim is released on refusal and failure; the agent's keep-alive ends
   on handoff, timeout, failure or agent stop, and the lease then lives only
   as long as the process keeps it.

## 10. Not built, and not verified

- Kubernetes discovery (DynamoWorkerMetadata), host-network workers and TLS
  etcd endpoints are refused.
- One identity per configuration: a second replica restored while the first
  holds the identity starts cold. A pool of identities per configuration is
  later work.
- A refused group restore is counted as a restore that did not run, which
  after three such refusals starts the configuration cold.
- Not run on hardware with this code: the network-namespace dialer, the
  net-unlock of a dump's lock and the probe through a Service address, and
  Dynamo's own keep-alive resuming on the granted lease after a CRIU restore
  (seen in a manual spike, not with this code).
